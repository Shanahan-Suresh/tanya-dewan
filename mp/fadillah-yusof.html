What are the main factors behind the recent sharp rise in domestic electricity charges?

Asked by Tuan Haji Azli bin Yusof [Shah Alam]

A (AI)

Higher global fuel prices from the West Asia geopolitical crisis, especially disruption in the Strait of Hormuz; prolonged hot weather raising air-conditioning use; and reduced generating capacity at some power plants, with more reliance on imported LNG.

Q (AI)

What is the current safety status and periodic monitoring of Linggiu Dam, and how effective is the dam safety flying squad in auditing its structural integrity?

Asked by Tuan Haji Manndzri bin Haji Nasib [Tenggara]

A (AI)

The dam is overseen by Singapore's PUB under the 1962 agreement and its 1990 supplementary agreement, with yearly and five-yearly inspections. The flying squad operated from 2018 to 2022 and inspected 41 dams. PEN, set up in 2025, monitors 107 dams, but Linggiu is outside its scope, so requests go through the Johor water regulatory body and PUB Singapore.

Q (AI)

What is the status of CRESS implementation and the number of industry participants, what was the outcome of the SAC rate review, and what are the implications for the data centre hub goal, DC operating costs, CGPP and BESS?

Asked by Puan Hajah Rodziah binti Ismail [Ampang]

A (AI)

Eleven CRESS developers have registered, involving eight green consumers, with about 3 gigawatts of renewable capacity. The SAC rate was reduced in August 2025 for firm and non-firm supply, which the minister said improves commercial viability and supports Malaysia as a green investment destination. CGPP was a pilot for third party access but has structural limits, so CRESS was introduced, and the MyBEST tender was launched for battery energy storage, which together support the 70 per cent renewable target by 2050.
