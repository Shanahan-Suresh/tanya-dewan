Q (AI)

What is the difference between the first and second ASEAN Haze-Free Roadmaps, and how are weaknesses addressed?

Asked by Dato’ Sri Tuan Ibrahim bin Tuan Man [Kubang Kerian]

A (AI)

The second roadmap adds a polluter pays principle, but the AATHP is cooperative and has no specific compensation-claim mechanism, so priority is strengthening it through diplomacy, information sharing and joint action. The vision is a haze-free ASEAN by 2030, with indicators such as fewer hotspots and burnt areas, fewer days at Alert Level 2 and 3, and no Alert Level 3 days for two consecutive years.

Answered by Dato’ Sri Arthur Joseph Kurup, Menteri Sumber Asli dan Kelestarian Alam

Figures given (4)
  • 2023–2030 Period of the second ASEAN Haze-Free Roadmap
  • 2030 Target year for a haze-free ASEAN
  • 2 dan 3 ASEAN Alert Levels whose number of days is to be reduced
  • dua Number of consecutive years with no Alert Level 3 days as a target

Q (AI)

Why was the 31 per cent NRW target for 2025 not achieved despite RM2.525 billion allocated under Budget 2025, and what immediate concrete steps will reach 25 per cent by 2030?

Asked by Datoʼ Sri Tuan Ibharim bin Tuan Man [Kubang Kerian]

A (AI)

Reducing NRW needs continuous, very large investment; tariffs not reflecting actual operating costs limited operators; COVID-19 delayed the programme; Cabinet approved an improved programme with RM2.525 billion for 2025 to 2030, with a pro-rata incentive and an NRW target of 28.8 per cent expected by 2030.

Answered by Transformasi Air [Datuk Amar Haji Fadillah bin Haji Yusof], Timbalan Perdana Menteri dan Menteri Peralihan Tenaga dan Transformasi Air

Figures given (5)
  • 31 peratus NRW target for 2025 under the Twelfth Malaysia Plan
  • RM2.525 bilion allocation approved by Cabinet for 2025 to 2030
  • 40 peratus NRW above 40 per cent in the programme states
  • 28.8 peratus NRW expected to be reached by 2030
  • 1 Ogos 2025 start date of the second tariff safety mechanism
