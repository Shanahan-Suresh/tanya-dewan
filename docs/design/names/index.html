Which name should the site use?

Here are 14 names, each shown as it would look in the site header. The three outlined cards are my shortlist. My pick is still Tanya Dewan: people can say it as a verb, and all three web addresses are free.

Tell me in chat which name you want. I can also mix ideas, for example a name plus a tagline.

Tanya Dewan

Shortlist pick 1 of 3

'Ask the House' (Dewan = the chamber/hall)

“Cuba tanya Dewan dulu”

Good
Says exactly what the site does; verb-first, imperative like Jom/Cuba; all three domains free.
Catch
'Dewan' alone also means any hall, and 'Dewan Rakyat' is the official chamber, so a little official-sounding; two words.
  • tanyadewan.myfree
  • tanyadewan.com.myfree
  • tanyadewan.comfree
Rekod Rakyat

Shortlist pick 2 of 3

'The People's Record'

“Check Rekod Rakyat”

Good
Neutral, civic, names the record idea; all three domains free.
Catch
Less of a verb, so it is harder to say 'I Rekod-Rakyat-ed it'; 'Rekod' alone suggests health records (Ministry of Health app RekodSaya exists) or criminal records; 'Rakyat' appears in many government apps (Respons Rakyat, Rakyat App).
  • rekodrakyat.myfree
  • rekodrakyat.com.myfree
  • rekodrakyat.comfree
Dewanku

Shortlist pick 3 of 3

'My House/Hall'

“Aku tengok kat Dewanku”

Good
Warm, personal, 'the House' idea; all three domains free.
Catch
Could read as an official parliament app (the official app is 'Parlimen Malaysia'); 'dewan' also means wedding/event hall so SEO noise; no meaning of asking.
  • dewanku.myfree
  • dewanku.com.myfree
  • dewanku.comfree
Bukti

'Proof, evidence'

“Mana buktinya? / I got it on Bukti”

Good
Short, punchy, matches 'receipts' hero line.
Catch
Sounds like a fact-check or expose site; .com taken.
  • bukti.myfree
  • bukti.com.myfree
  • bukti.comtaken
Semak

'Check, review'

“Semak kat Semak”

Good
Very short, a real everyday verb for checking.
Catch
Slang 'semak' = cluttered/annoying; semak.com taken; MySPR Semak (election commission voter-check app) exists, so semantically close to an official service.
  • semak.mytaken
  • semak.com.myfree
  • semak.comtaken
Rujuk

'Refer to, consult'

“Rujuk je kat Rujuk”

Good
Everyday word for looking something up, ideal for students/journalists; .my and .com.my free.
Catch
In Malay family law 'rujuk' = reconciling with a divorced spouse (religious connotation); generic; rujuk.com taken.
  • rujuk.myfree
  • rujuk.com.myfree
  • rujuk.comtaken
Petik

'Quote, cite' (also to pick/pluck)

“Petik dari Petik”

Good
Nails the cite-every-sentence idea.
Catch
Also means 'to pluck/pick' and appears in many small business names; petik.my and petik.com taken.
  • petik.mytaken
  • petik.com.myfree
  • petik.comtaken
Lantai

'The floor' (as in 'on the floor of the House'; 'lantai' = floor)

“Apa dia cakap kat Lantai?”

Good
Short, evocative of the debating floor; .my free.
Catch
Mostly known as plain 'floor' (tiles/flooring businesses), so confusing; lantai.com taken; says nothing about asking.
  • lantai.myfree
  • lantai.com.myfree
  • lantai.comtaken
Bicara

'Speak, discuss, hear (a case)'

“Cari kat Bicara”

Good
Short, pleasant, covers debate.
Catch
Very common word; 'bicara' also means a court trial/hearing (legal tone) and there are many Bicara-named talk shows/podcasts/brands; bicara.com taken.
  • bicara.myfree
  • bicara.com.myfree
  • bicara.comtaken
Catat

'Note down, record'

“Catat dalam Catat”

Good
Short, 'the record' idea.
Catch
Meaning is writing things down, not asking; catat.my and catat.com taken (existing brand likely).
  • catat.mytaken
  • catat.com.myfree
  • catat.comtaken
Soalan

'Question'

“Hantar soalan kat Soalan”

Good
Instantly clear to everyone; .my and .com.my free.
Catch
Generic and confused with exam question banks (soalan SPM sites) and with Parliament's own 'soalan lisan'; soalan.com taken.
  • soalan.myfree
  • soalan.com.myfree
  • soalan.comtaken
Ask Hansard

Plain-English 'ask the parliamentary record'

“I asked Ask Hansard”

Good
Clear in English; Hansard is the word journalists and students know.
Catch
English only and longer. “Hansard” may puzzle casual Malay readers.
  • askhansard.myfree
  • askhansard.com.myfree
  • askhansard.comtaken
Tanya Hansard

'Ask the Hansard'

“Cuba Tanya Hansard”

Good
Blend of Malay verb and the English term; all three domains free; stronger tie to the record than Tanya Dewan.
Catch
Three-plus syllables heavier; 'Hansard' reads as official; the official Digital Hansard portal (hansard.parlimen.gov.my, relaunched June 2026) is similar-sounding so mistaking is possible.
  • tanyahansard.myfree
  • tanyahansard.com.myfree
  • tanyahansard.comfree
Cakna

'Care about, be aware'

“Kena cakna sikit”

Good
Warm, distinctly Malaysian, good tone.
Catch
Does not say asking or the record; used widely as a word and brand (cakna.com taken; Cakna campaigns exist); weak link to function.
  • cakna.mytaken
  • cakna.com.myfree
  • cakna.comtaken
How the names were checked

Web addresses were checked on 26 November 2026. The .com addresses were checked with Verisign's lookup service. The .my and .com.my addresses were checked with MYNIC, which runs Malaysian domains. Nothing was registered or reserved.

A few web searches looked for Malaysian apps, sites or groups with the same name and found none. That search was light, so treat it as a first look, not a clearance. Trademarks and social media handles are not checked yet; I check them for the name you pick.
