What happened

Sim Tze Tzin (Bayan Baru) said diesel subsidy spending has risen to RM14 billion from about RM8 billion in 2019 and 2020, that blanket subsidies are not sustainable, and that targeting still gives diesel subsidy to farmers, needy groups and 400,000 individuals. Several members, including Datuk Awang bin Hashim (Pendang), Haji Mohd Syahir bin Che Sulaiman (Bachok) and Lee Chean Chung (Petaling Jaya), repeatedly asked to interject, and the Speaker repeatedly asked them to sit. Sim Tze Tzin said former prime ministers had said the subsidy was not sustainable and accused the opposition of hypocrisy, while Dr Radzi bin Jidin (Putrajaya) asked whether the promise to lower oil prices was made to win applause. Datuk Dr Ahmad Marzuk bin Shaary (Pengkalan Chepa) said the government was deceiving the people, and Datuk Awang bin Hashim said the current Prime Minister had promised to lower oil prices, which Sim Tze Tzin disputed, stating that the Pakatan Harapan, Barisan Nasional and GPS manifestos never promised this and referring to the 2008 manifesto. Sim Tze Tzin also said Malaysia had become a net importer of oil, with production falling from 500,000 to 300,000 barrels per day, Dato' Rosol bin Wahid (Hulu Terengganu) said Sim was living on Mars, and Datuk Awang bin Hashim began to ask about the price per barrel compared with 2018 but was cut off. Sim Tze Tzin then called on the Minister to enforce action against those taking advantage to raise prices, Hassan bin Abdul Karim (Pasir Gudang) asked to defend the government's decision, Syed Saddiq bin Syed Abdul Rahman (Muar) stood, and the Speaker said time was up and invited the Minister of Health, Datuk Seri Dr. Dzulkefly bin Ahmad, to reply.
